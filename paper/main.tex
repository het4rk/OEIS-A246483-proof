\title{A proof of Hardin's empirical row recurrences for OEIS A246479 (A246483--A246486), formalized in Lean~4}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let $T(n,k)$ be the number of arrays of length $n+3$ over $\{0,\dots,k\}$ with no two entries among any four consecutive terms summing to $k$ (OEIS A246479~\cite{oeis}). For fixed $n$ (a ``row''), R.~H.~Hardin recorded linear recurrences in $k$ as ``Empirical'': explicitly for $n=1,2$ in A246479 and for $n=4,\dots,7$ in A246483--A246486, and by order only ($11,13,\dots,19$) for $n=3,\dots,7$. We prove that for $1\le n\le 7$ the row is a quasi-polynomial $T(n,k)=A_n(k)+(-1)^kB_n(k)$ with $\deg A_n=n+3$ and $\deg B_n=n$, give $A_n$ and $B_n$ explicitly, and deduce all of these recurrences, which have characteristic polynomial $(1-x)^{n+4}(1+x)^{n+1}$. The proof is machine-checked in Lean~4 with Mathlib~\cite{mathlib}: no \texttt{sorry}, no \texttt{native\_decide}, only the axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}. We conjecture the same structure for every $n$ and check it numerically for $n\le 40$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and related work}
OEIS A246479 (Hardin, 2014) lists ``Empirical for row $n$'' recurrences: for $n=1$, $a(k)=3a(k-1)-a(k-2)-5a(k-3)+5a(k-4)+a(k-5)-3a(k-6)+a(k-7)$; for $n=2$ an order-$9$ recurrence; and ``order $11,13,15,17,19$'' for $n=3,\dots,7$. Rows $4$--$7$ are the separate entries A246483--A246486, each with an explicit ``Empirical'' recurrence of order $2n+5$~\cite{oeisrows}. For rows $1$--$3$ (A246480--A246482), Colin Barker conjectured quasi-polynomial closed forms in 2018; these, and the column recurrences of A246479, were proved in our earlier work~\cite{a246479}, which also supplies the transfer-matrix reduction to eleven triple types used here. No proof of the row recurrences for $n\ge4$, or of closed forms for these rows, was found in the OEIS or by a web search on October 12, 2026; all of them are marked empirical.

That each row is \emph{some} quasi-polynomial in $k$ is a standard consequence of the transfer-matrix description (the matrix entries are affine in $k$ for each parity of $k$; cf.\ Stanley~\cite{stanley}, \S4.4 and \S4.7). The content here is the exact degrees, in particular $\deg B_n=n$ for the alternating part, which is what makes Hardin's recurrences have order exactly $2n+5$, together with explicit formulas and a machine-checked proof.

\section{Results}
Write $P_n^{\mathrm e}$ and $P_n^{\mathrm o}$ for the polynomials with $T(n,k)=P_n^{\mathrm e}(k)$ for even $k$ and $T(n,k)=P_n^{\mathrm o}(k)$ for odd $k$, and $A_n=\tfrac12(P^{\mathrm e}_n+P^{\mathrm o}_n)$, $B_n=\tfrac12(P^{\mathrm e}_n-P^{\mathrm o}_n)$.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rows}
For $n=4,\dots,7$ and all $k\ge0$ (including $k=0$):
\begin{align*}
P_4^{\mathrm e}&=k^7-8k^6+36k^5-97k^4+166k^3-167k^2+76k,\\
P_4^{\mathrm o}&=k^7-8k^6+36k^5-84k^4+93k^3+16k^2-129k+77,\\
P_5^{\mathrm e}&=k^8-10k^7+55k^6-190k^5+442k^4-683k^3+642k^2-277k,\\
P_5^{\mathrm o}&=k^8-10k^7+55k^6-174k^5+322k^4-256k^3-150k^2+441k-227,\\
P_6^{\mathrm e}&=k^9-12k^8+78k^7-327k^6+955k^5-1969k^4+2777k^3-2415k^2+972k,\\
P_6^{\mathrm o}&=k^9-12k^8+78k^7-308k^6+776k^5-1144k^4+623k^3+796k^2-1477k+669,\\
P_7^{\mathrm e}&=k^{10}-14k^9+105k^8-516k^7+1805k^6-4619k^5+8596k^4-11118k^3+8948k^2-3356k,\\
P_7^{\mathrm o}&=k^{10}-14k^9+105k^8-494k^7+1555k^6-3206k^5+3837k^4-1162k^3-3525k^2+4877k-1972.
\end{align*}
For $n=1,2,3$ they are Barker's formulas~\cite{a246479}. In each case $\deg A_n=n+3$ and $\deg B_n=n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:rec}
For $1\le n\le7$, with $a(k)=T(n,k)$ and $(1-x)^{n+4}(1+x)^{n+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{2n+5}p_ix^i$, we have $\sum_i p_i\,a(k-i)=0$ for all $k\ge 2n+5$. This is exactly Hardin's recurrence for $n=1,2$ (A246479) and $n=4,\dots,7$ (A246483--A246486), coefficient by coefficient, and gives the recurrences of orders $11,\dots,19$ that A246479 lists for $n=3,\dots,7$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$(1-E^{-1})^{n+4}$ annihilates polynomials of degree $\le n+3$, and $(1+E^{-1})^{n+1}$ annihilates $(-1)^kB(k)$ for $\deg B\le n$.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rows}}
We use the reduction of~\cite{a246479}: with $\varepsilon=[k\text{ even}]$ there is a vector $V_n\in\mathbb Z^{11}$ of type values, $V_0=\mathbf 1$, $V_{n+1}=M_{k,\varepsilon}V_n$, and $T(n,k)=F_{k,\varepsilon}(V_n)$, where the entries of $M$ are affine in $k$ and $\varepsilon$ and $F$ is a cubic form in $k,\varepsilon$ (Lean: \lean{step}, \lean{F}, \lean{T\_eq\_F}). Fixing $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$, we write out the vectors $V_0,\dots,V_7$ with entries in $\mathbb Z[k]$ (\lean{We0}--\lean{We7}, \lean{Wo0}--\lean{Wo7}), check $V_{n+1}=MV_n$ componentwise, and evaluate $F$. All of these are polynomial identities checked by \texttt{ring}. Combining the two parities gives $2T(n,k)=2A_n(k)+(-1)^k\,2B_n(k)$ (\lean{row}$n$\lean{\_quasi}), and the recurrences follow by substituting this into both sides (\lean{row}$n$\lean{\_rec}).

\textbf{Numerical checks.} The recursion reproduces all $9999$ terms of Hardin's b-file for A246479, and the closed forms were also checked against brute-force enumeration for $n\le3$, $k\le4$.

\begin{conjecture}
For every $n\ge1$, $\deg A_n=n+3$ and $\deg B_n=n$; hence row $n$ satisfies the recurrence with characteristic polynomial $(1-x)^{n+4}(1+x)^{n+1}$.
\end{conjecture}
We verified this for $1\le n\le 40$ and $0\le k\le 2n+29$ (\texttt{scripts/check\_bfile.py}). The degree bound $\deg A_n\le n+3$ is immediate; the content is the bound on $B_n$. Numerically, every entry of $V_L$ has degree $\le L$ in $k$ while its dependence on $\varepsilon$ has degree $\le L-3$, which suggests an inductive proof, but we have not completed one.

\section{Limitations}
The formal results cover $n\le7$, which are all the rows for which the OEIS states a recurrence; the general-$n$ statement is a conjecture supported by computation. The proof is a direct computation on top of the transfer-matrix reduction of~\cite{a246479}; its value is in turning these empirical formulas into theorems with explicit closed forms and a machine-checked proof.
